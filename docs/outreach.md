# Bolton Trades — outreach pack

Copy-paste messages for the consent round and the launch push. Swap `[LINK]` for the demo URL.

A note on all of these: they are short on purpose. A long message in a WhatsApp group gets skimmed and forgotten. Resist the urge to add detail — the detail is what the follow-up conversation is for.

---

## 1. To the group — first announcement

> Morning all. I've put together something for this group.
>
> Every week someone asks for a recommendation and we all reply from memory. I've built a simple app where you can look a trader up and see what people actually said about them — did they turn up, did they stick to the quote, would you use them again.
>
> Have a look: [LINK]
>
> The traders and reviews in it are made up for now, just to show how it works. If people think it's worth doing I'll set it up properly with our lot.
>
> Free for everyone. Tell me if it's rubbish.

**Why it's worded this way:** it asks for a reaction, not permission. "Tell me if it's rubbish" gets far more replies than "let me know what you think," and the replies are more honest.

---

## 2. To each trader, individually — consent

Send this one-to-one. Not as a broadcast, not in the group. Response rates are not remotely comparable.

> Alright [Name], it's Yameen from the Bolton Tradesmen group.
>
> I'm setting up a small directory for the group so people can find a trader and see honest feedback rather than scrolling back through the chat. I'd like to list you.
>
> If you're happy with it I'd put up: your name, your trade, the areas you cover, and a button people tap to ring or WhatsApp you. Your number isn't shown on the page — it sits behind the button.
>
> The bit to be straight with you about: people who've used you can leave reviews, and I won't take one down because it's unflattering. Only if it's fake, abusive, or not about a real job. That cuts both ways — it's what makes a good rating worth having.
>
> Costs you nothing. You can pull out any time and I'll take you off within a day.
>
> Yes or no?

**Do not soften the middle paragraph.** A trader who agrees without understanding the review policy is the trader who rings you angry in month three. Getting a "no" now is cheaper than getting a fight later.

---

## 3. The commitment test

Only send this after they've said yes to (2) and seen the demo. This is the §11 pre-build gate — the answer decides whether you write any more code.

> Quick one. Down the line I want to add a verified badge — I'd check your public liability, your ID and your trade registration, and the badge would show what I'd checked and when it runs out. Roughly £3-4 a month, billed yearly.
>
> To be clear about what it does and doesn't do: it doesn't push you up the list. Nothing moves you up the list except your reviews. It just shows people you're covered and you're who you say you are.
>
> I'm not charging anyone until the thing's actually sending you work. But I need to know if it's worth building.
>
> If you'd pay for that, send me £10 now and I'll knock it off your first year — or give it you back if I don't build it. If you'd rather wait and see, that's a fair answer too, just tell me.

**Read the result honestly.** Ten deposits means build it. Three deposits and a lot of "sounds good mate" means you have goodwill, not demand — and goodwill does not renew.

---

## 4. Asking someone for a review

Send individually, to people you know had work done. A broadcast asking for reviews gets about a 5% response; a personal one gets 40%.

> Hiya [Name] — didn't [Trader] do your [job] a few months back? I've got that trades app up and it'd help me a lot if you stuck a review on. Five questions, takes a minute: [LINK]
>
> Be honest with it — if it was a mixed job, say so. It's no use to anyone if everyone's got five stars.

That last line matters more than the rest of the message. Say it every single time.

## 5. Your reply in the group, from now on

Every time someone asks for a recommendation, reply with this. Every time, for at least three months. This is the actual work.

Then let other people add their usual replies underneath. Don't argue for the app, just keep putting it where the question is.

## 6. Consent form wording

The form the trader taps through from message 2. Keep it to one screen.

**Fields:** name · trade (max 3) · areas covered · mobile · WhatsApp number if different · email (optional, for the monthly stats)

**The statement they tick — do not shorten this:**

> I agree to Bolton Trades listing my name, trade, areas covered and a contact button on its public directory.
>
> I understand that people who have used me can post reviews about my work, that reviews are published without being approved by me first, and that Bolton Trades will not remove a review simply because I disagree with it.
>
> I understand I can withdraw at any time and be removed from the directory within one working day. If I withdraw, my reviews are hidden with my profile — and if I list again later, they come back with it.
>
> I have read the privacy notice.

**Below the button, in small text:**

> Bolton Trades is run by [your name / company]. We're the data controller for the information above. Contact: [email].

That last line is not optional — a consent that doesn't say who's collecting the data isn't valid consent.

---

## 7. Things not to say

- **"It's like Checkatrade but free."** Invites the comparison you lose. You're not a bigger directory; you're the only one where the reviews come from people they know.
- **"Get more customers."** You cannot promise that yet and if you say it, month three is awkward. Promise a listing and honest feedback. Nothing else.
- **"Pay to be featured."** Never, in any message, in any form.
- **Anything about a launch date.** You'll miss it, and in a small group missing it publicly costs you more than saying nothing.

---

## 8. Order of operations

1. Post message 1. Wait a week. Count the replies and the demo opens.
2. Send message 2 to every trader in the group, individually. Target 25 yeses.
3. Send message 3 to everyone who said yes. **Count the deposits — this is the go/no-go.**
4. Build only if step 3 passes.
5. Message 4 to everyone you know has had work done. Target 60 reviews in four weeks.
6. Message 5, every time, for three months.

Steps 1–3 cost you nothing but a few evenings on your phone, and they answer the only question that matters.
